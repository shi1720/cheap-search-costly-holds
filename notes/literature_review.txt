LITERATURE AND CONTRIBUTION AUDIT
Study: Cheap Search, Costly Holds: Reservation Synchronization in Agentic Markets
Search date: 16 October 2026

Question selected
How do exclusive temporary alternatives affect distinct successful transactions when delegated search becomes cheaper? The study separates attempted breadth, hold duration, and cross-supplier release dependence. Its scientific claims concern declared mathematical models and constructed events, not observed marketplace prevalence.

Searches covered AI procurement, agentic commerce, duplicate bookings, multiple applications, strategic queueing and multi-listing, reservation externalities, batch admission, synchronized departures, loss networks, redundancy dependence, and congestion pricing. Screen against earlier local manuscripts
The opening two pages of all 53 top-level PDFs present in Downloads/research were extracted for topic screening. The nearest earlier manuscripts are:

No figures, results, code, data, or proofs from these manuscripts were reused. This audit concerns topic and contribution overlap, not the publication status or quality of the earlier papers.

Defensible contribution claims
A. A finite dynamic construction matches the complete one-supplier pre-commitment reservation-state process under two release mechanisms and derives an unbounded ratio of distinct successful mandates. Final-purchase labels and cross-supplier linkage are explicitly excluded from the matched observation.
B. A conditional cheap-search asymptotic specifies constants and rates for equilibrium breadth, success, and net welfare when hold duration grows as a power of breadth. Constant duration is a countercase.
C. An exhaustive symmetric-equilibrium solver, a scalar-LP planner with interval upper bounds, and an occupancy supporting price specialize established methods to that model.
D. Exact and event-driven checks retain finite correlations and expose a startup synchronization artifact; long-warm-up follow-up is labeled exploratory and reported separately.

What is not claimed
The paper does not introduce multiple-application externalities, the general inadequacy of marginals, Pigouvian pricing, or loss-network balance. It does not prove general large-market convergence, field prevalence, deployable commercial improvement, universal originality, patentability, or likely acceptance at a top AI conference. No finite literature search establishes that no similar unpublished or inaccessible result exists. No exact duplicate of claims A-B was located in the reviewed material; this remains a qualified literature-search conclusion.

Suitability
This is a theoretical/computational economics manuscript aligned with Econburgh's AI/economy, digital markets, quantitative methods, and industrial-organization topics. Its economics venue fit is stronger than a claim of a new LLM architecture or experimentally evaluated AI capability.
